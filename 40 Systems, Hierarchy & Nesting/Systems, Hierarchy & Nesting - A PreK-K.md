# Systems, Hierarchy & Nesting — Band A (Pre-K–K, ages 3–5)

**Core idea:** things can fit inside other things, and things can be sorted into groups.

**Spiral note:** first pass. Pure physical/sensory play with nesting and sorting; no "hierarchy" vocabulary yet.

## Hands-on activities
- Stacking cups or Russian nesting dolls — put the small one inside the big one, over and over.
- Sort toys by size (big/small) or by kind (animals/vehicles) into separate bins.
- A toy box inside a closet inside a bedroom — notice "inside of inside of inside."
- "Which one is bigger, which one goes inside which" guessing games.

## Vocabulary

Words this band uses, defined the way we'd describe them to someone who'd never seen them — what happens, not how it feels:

- **Inside** — fully surrounded by something else, with nothing of it sticking out.
- **Group** — a set of things put together because they share something.
- **Sort** — putting each thing into the group it matches.
- **Bigger / smaller** — holding more or less space than something else.

Held back for later: *nesting* (the pattern of inside-inside-inside, repeating) and *category* (a named kind of group) — both arrive by name in [[Systems, Hierarchy & Nesting - B Grades 1-2]]. This band names only what she can touch and see directly; the pattern gets its word once she's noticed it enough times to want one.

## Draft

### Nesting Cups

Give Mila a set of stacking cups or nesting dolls and just let her play — open the biggest, find the next one inside, and so on down. Resist naming anything at first; let her hands find the pattern. Once she's done it a few times, turn it into a guessing game: hand her a cup and ask "do you think something fits inside this one, or is this the smallest?" before she opens it. The felt pattern — big holds the next-size-down, which holds the next — is the entire lesson at this age; the word for it comes later.

### Sorting Into Bins

Give her a mixed pile of toys and two or three empty bins, and ask her to put them away "her way" — by size, by kind, by color, whatever she reaches for. Don't suggest the categories. Once she's sorted one way, dump them back out and ask her to sort them a second, different way, to show her there isn't just one right grouping — the same toy can belong to more than one group depending on what you're sorting by.

### Boxes Inside Boxes

Walk around the house and find a real something-inside-something-inside-something — a toy box inside her closet inside her bedroom is the easiest one. Trace it out loud together, pointing at each layer: "the toy is inside the box, the box is inside the closet, the closet is inside your room." Ask if she can find another one on her own. This noticing — that "inside of inside of inside" shows up everywhere, not just in her toy bin — is exactly what gets a name (nesting, categories) once she's at [[Systems, Hierarchy & Nesting - B Grades 1-2]].
