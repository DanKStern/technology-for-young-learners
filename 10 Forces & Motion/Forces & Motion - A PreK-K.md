# Forces & Motion — Band A (Pre-K–K, ages 3–5)

**Core idea:** things fall down, and things slow down when they rub against each other. No names yet — just notice it.

**Spiral note:** first pass. Nothing to build on yet; this band plants the raw sensory experience that Band B will name.

## Hands-on activities
- Drop things — a ball, a block, a feather — and watch what happens. Which falls faster? Does anything go up by itself?
- Push a toy car across carpet, then across a smooth floor, then across sandpaper. Which goes farthest?
- Race two balls down a ramp at the same time — one smooth, one covered in cloth.
- Vocabulary stays at "push," "pull," "fast," "slow," "stop" — no "gravity" or "friction" yet.

## Draft

### Dropping Things

Gather a handful of different objects — a ball, a wooden block, a feather, maybe a sheet of paper — and let Mila drop them one at a time from the same height. Before each drop, ask her: "What do you think this one will do?" Don't tell her anything yet; her prediction is the whole point at this age. After each drop, ask what happened — did it go down fast or slow? The feather and paper will noticeably drift and flutter instead of dropping straight down, which is worth lingering on rather than smoothing over: "Look how that one took its time." No answer is owed yet for *why* — this band is pure noticing.

Once she's seen several, try tossing something straight up and watching it come back down on its own, nothing holding it. That one is worth repeating a few times — toss, watch, land — because at this age repetition does the work an explanation would do later.

### Racing Down the Ramp

Prop a board on a book to make a simple ramp, and cover half of it in cloth or a towel. Let her feel the texture difference with her hand first — smooth side, then rough side — so the slowdown isn't a surprise divorced from a cause she can feel. Then race two identical balls down the two halves at the same time, asking "which one do you think will win?" before letting go.

Switch to the toy car next: push it across carpet, then a smooth floor, then sandpaper if you have it, asking each time "how far do you think it'll go?" before pushing with about the same strength.

Close by naming what's happening in only the words this band allows: "Some ground holds on to things more, some ground lets them go further." No "friction" yet — that word, and the word for the falling thing too, are waiting for her at [[Forces & Motion - B Grades 1-2]].
