# Life & Living Systems — Band A (Pre-K–K, ages 3–5)

**Core idea:** some things are alive and some aren't, and living things need things (food, water, sun) to stay alive.

**Spiral note:** first pass. Pure sorting and caretaking experience; no "biology" vocabulary yet.

## Hands-on activities
- Sort a mixed pile of pictures/objects (rock, dog, car, flower, cloud) into "alive" and "not alive," and talk through the surprising ones.
- Plant a bean or sunflower seed and water it daily; watch it change over days.
- Care for a classroom pet or houseplant and name what it needs that a toy doesn't.
- Go on a "look for living things" walk outside.

## Vocabulary

Words this band uses, defined the way we'd describe them to someone who'd never seen them — what happens, not how it feels:

- **Alive** — able to grow and needs things like water, food, or sunlight to keep going.
- **Grow** — getting bigger or changing shape over days or weeks, on its own.
- **Need** — something a thing must keep getting, or it stops living.

Held back for later: *life cycle* (the named stages a living thing passes through as it grows) — arrives in [[Life & Living Systems - B Grades 1-2]]. This band only notices that living things change over time; the word for the pattern that change follows waits until she's watched one all the way through.

## Draft

### Alive or Not?

Gather a mixed pile of pictures or small objects — a toy dog, a real leaf, a rock, a photo of a car, a stuffed animal, a flower — and ask Mila to sort them into "alive" and "not alive" herself before you weigh in, including the hard ones (a stuffed animal that looks alive but isn't; a seed that looks like a rock but will grow). Save the "why" for the ones she gets stuck on or that surprise her: "What made you put the rock in this pile?" The goal isn't a perfect sort — it's noticing that the line isn't always obvious at a glance, a tension later bands actually get to resolve (see [[Life & Living Systems - B Grades 1-2]] for the first real clue: living things grow through stages).

### Growing Something

Plant a bean or sunflower seed together in a cup where she can see it, and make watering it her own daily routine rather than yours. Each day, before watering, ask what's different from yesterday — let her look before she waters, not after. Over the week or two it takes to sprout, she's building the central Band A idea of this strand: living things change over time in ways a rock or a toy never will, because they need things (water, light) to keep going. Keep this cup alive past this lesson if you can — it's the same cup the life-cycle lesson in [[Life & Living Systems - B Grades 1-2]] comes back to.

### What Does It Need?

Put a houseplant (or a pet, if you have one) next to one of her toys and ask directly: "What does this need that your toy doesn't?" Let her list things herself — water, food, sunlight, air — and test a couple on the toy for laughs ("does your teddy bear need water?"). Finish with a short "living things walk" outside or around the house, pointing at anything she thinks might be alive and asking her to say why, before you confirm or correct.
