# Matter & Energy — Band D (Grades 6–8, ages 11–13)

**Core idea:** the periodic table is a map of all the different kinds of atoms, chemical reactions rearrange atoms into new molecules (with measurable evidence), and energy just keeps changing form, never disappearing.

**Spiral note:** builds on [[Matter & Energy - C Grades 3-5]] — atoms/molecules and physical-vs-chemical change are already familiar; this band adds the periodic table as the catalog of atom types and ties energy transformation explicitly back to [[Forces & Motion - D Grades 6-8]]'s energy-conservation idea (chemical energy → heat → motion, same "energy changes form" principle either direction).

## Hands-on activities
- Look at a real periodic table and find familiar elements (oxygen, carbon, iron); point out it's organized by pattern, not alphabetically.
- Run a reaction with clear before/after evidence (temperature change, color change, gas bubbles) and identify what evidence proves a *new* substance formed.
- Trace one energy story end-to-end: a battery's chemical energy → light-bulb heat and light, or food's chemical energy → body heat and motion.
- Revisit the Band C ball-and-stick water model and extend it to a slightly bigger molecule (CO₂) to show the same building-block idea scales up.
- Revisit the Band B radiometer demo from the energy side: the black vane absorbs light and converts it to heat, and that heat energy becomes the mechanical energy of spinning — a real, visible example of one form of energy converting into another, same principle as [[Forces & Motion - D Grades 6-8]]'s energy-conservation story.

## Draft

### Reading the Periodic Table

Look at a real periodic table together and find a few familiar elements — oxygen, carbon, iron, maybe gold or helium. Ask: "why do you think it's arranged this way instead of just alphabetical?" It's organized by pattern (how the atoms are built, which drives how they behave), not by name — elements in the same column tend to behave similarly, the way the ball-and-stick water model from [[Matter & Energy - C Grades 3-5]] showed hydrogen and oxygen combining in a fixed, patterned way rather than randomly.

### Proving Something New Formed

Run a reaction with clear, visible evidence — a color change, a temperature change, gas bubbles (baking soda and vinegar again works) — and ask the child to list every piece of evidence that proves a *new* substance formed rather than the old ones just mixing. This is the Band C physical-vs-chemical distinction, but now the child builds the evidence case themselves instead of being told the answer.

### One Energy Story, Start to Finish

Pick one real energy chain and trace every step out loud: a battery's stored chemical energy becomes a lightbulb's heat and light, or the food on a breakfast plate becomes the energy for a whole afternoon of running around. At each step ask "where did that energy come from, and where's it going next?" The point to land on: energy isn't created at any of these steps, it's converted — a direct echo of [[Forces & Motion - D Grades 6-8]]'s "friction doesn't destroy energy, it turns it into heat" finding, just traced through a longer chain.

### From Water to Carbon Dioxide

Pull the ball-and-stick water model back out, then build CO₂ next to it — one bigger ball (carbon) with two smaller balls (oxygen) attached on either side, a straight line instead of water's bent shape. Ask the child to compare: same building-block idea, different atoms, different shape, different substance entirely. A few kinds of building blocks making endless different things depending on how they're combined is the real payoff of the atoms idea, not just a vocabulary word.

### The Radiometer's Energy Story

Run the radiometer once more, and narrate the whole energy conversion as one chain, same structure as the "one energy story" activity above: light hits the black side of each vane, the black side absorbs it and heats up (the Band B black-vs-white finding, finally getting its reason), that heat becomes the kinetic energy of the surrounding gas molecules, and that becomes the mechanical energy of the vanes spinning. Ask the child to say the whole chain back in their own words, light to spin, without supplying any of the links yourself.
