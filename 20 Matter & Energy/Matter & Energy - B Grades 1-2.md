# Matter & Energy — Band B (Grades 1–2, ages 6–7)

**Core idea:** matter comes in three states — solid, liquid, gas — and some changes can be undone (melting ice) while others can't (baking a cookie).

**Spiral note:** builds on [[Matter & Energy - A PreK-K]] — the child has already watched water change form; this band names the three states and introduces "can I undo this?" as the test for reversible vs. non-reversible change.

## Hands-on activities
- Sort pictures (or real objects) into solid/liquid/gas bins; name each state.
- Dissolve sugar or salt in water, then try to get it back by evaporating the water — reversible.
- Bake a simple cookie or pancake and ask: can we turn this back into batter? — non-reversible.
- Revisit the ice-water-steam cycle from Band A and now give each stage its name.
- Shine a flashlight on the black side of a Crookes radiometer's vanes vs. the white side — the black side heats up faster and that's what makes it spin. Compare to a black vs. white piece of paper left in the sun: which one feels warmer? This connects "light turns into heat" to something the child can feel directly. Revisited with the real mechanism in [[Matter & Energy - D Grades 6-8]].
- Growl low and squeak high into [[Voice Scope]] and compare the wiggles: low sounds make slow, wide wiggles; high sounds make fast, bunched-up ones. Back it up with a ruler twanged off the edge of a table — shorter ruler, faster shake, higher sound.
- Record a word with [[Voice Scope]], then switch its four animal buttons on and off to hear each part of the voice alone and mixed back together — a sound that *can* be un-mixed, unlike the paint from Band A.
- Watch a steady piano note make an even wiggle, then compare a few different keys across an octave — higher keys pack the wiggle tighter.
- Hear three separate notes one at a time, then slide smoothly between pitches with a dial — notice how jumping between them is different from sliding.

### Fast Wiggles, Slow Wiggles

Open [[Voice Scope]] and have her growl as low as she can, like a bear, while you both watch the bottom screen. Then have her squeak as high as she can, like a mouse. Ask before explaining anything: "what's different about the wiggles?" Let her look as long as she wants — the low growl makes slow, wide waves; the squeak packs them in tight. (Tip: a steady hum or a sung "oooo" makes much cleaner wiggles than ordinary talking does.)

Now give it a name. Sound is something shaking back and forth — a vibration — and her throat is what's shaking, the same buzz she may have felt with her fingers at [[Matter & Energy - A PreK-K#Seeing Your Voice|Band A]]. How *fast* it shakes is what makes a sound high or low. To prove it's not just a voice thing, hold a ruler flat on a table with most of it hanging off the edge and twang it: a long overhang wobbles slowly and makes a low sound; slide it back so less hangs off, and it shakes faster and the sound goes up. "Is the ruler doing the same thing your throat does?"

### Which Animal Lives in Your Voice?

Introduce the four animal buttons on [[Voice Scope]] — Bear, Person, Bird, Snake — as four parts hiding inside every voice, from the deepest to the hissiest. Record her saying "sssssnake." Play it with all four on, then turn everything off except Snake and play it again: it's almost nothing but a hiss. Ask: "where did the rest of your voice go?" Now record "boom" and try Bear alone — the boom is still there, just deep and muffled, like hearing it through a wall.

Then try the reverse: turn on all four, then switch off *only* Snake, and ask whether she can still understand the word. Let her switch the animals on and off herself while it loops — that's the part that's fun. The idea to land on, tying back to this band's "can I undo this?" question: when she mixed paint at [[Matter & Energy - A PreK-K#Mixing Colors|Band A]], she couldn't pull the colors back apart — but a voice is a mix that *can* be taken apart and put back together, one animal at a time. Which animals matter most for understanding words comes back at [[Information & Communication - C Grades 3-5#Which Part Carries the Words?|Band C]]. Stop while she's still having fun.

### Piano Pictures

*(Needs a piano/tone input and a digital frequency readout on [[Voice Scope]] — not built yet.)*

Play a single note on a real piano (or a toy keyboard) next to the microphone and watch the wiggle on the bottom screen settle into a steady, even pattern — unlike a voice, which wobbles. Play a few different keys across one octave, one at a time, and ask "what do you think a higher key's wiggle will look like?" before each one plays. Once she's noticed higher keys pack the wiggles tighter — the same noticing as her own growl-vs-squeak at [[Matter & Energy - B Grades 1-2#Fast Wiggles, Slow Wiggles|Fast Wiggles, Slow Wiggles]] — show her the number next to the picture, so she has something to point to besides "that one looks squished."

### Step by Step, Then Smooth

*(Needs an adjustable tone generator on [[Voice Scope]] — not built yet.)*

Starting from a single steady tone, let her hear and see three notes one at a time, spaced apart rather than right next to each other. Ask what she notices changing between them each time. Then switch to a slider and let her drag it slowly herself, watching the wiggle squeeze and stretch continuously as the sound glides. Ask: "was jumping between the three notes different from sliding?" No reaching for words like discrete or continuous here — the noticing is the point, not the vocabulary.
